## Executive Summary

This report presents a structured crisis communication and stakeholder management response to a suspected ransomware-related outage occurring during a time-critical payroll week. At 08:15 on Monday, a critical shared-file environment became unavailable to several teams. A ransomware incident is one possibility under investigation, an unverified screenshot is circulating among employees, and payroll is scheduled for Friday.

The response prioritizes prompt communication without converting assumptions into organizational claims. It establishes a single source of truth, defines approval and ownership responsibilities, maps stakeholder information needs, provides audience-specific message templates, and introduces resilient communication channels for circumstances in which corporate systems may be unavailable or untrusted.

The central communication strategy is to classify information as **confirmed**, **under investigation**, or **not yet known**. This approach enables the organization to communicate the operational disruption immediately while withholding unverified statements about the attacker, entry point, data exposure, incident scope, recovery time, regulatory obligations, and payroll outcome.

---

## 1. Incident Context

At 08:15 on Monday, the organization identified an outage affecting access to a critical file share used by several teams. The security team began preserving evidence and investigating the cause. Although ransomware is being considered, it has not been confirmed.

The incident creates several immediate concerns:

- Operational disruption across teams that depend on shared files.
- Possible delay to Friday payroll if critical information remains unavailable.
- Loss or contamination of forensic evidence through unauthorized recovery attempts.
- Misinformation arising from an unverified screenshot circulating in staff group chats.
- Potential customer concern before the scope of impact is established.
- Possible legal, regulatory, or contractual obligations that require assessment.
- Increased fraud risk through false payment or bank-detail change instructions.

The organization must therefore communicate quickly, consistently, and cautiously while the technical investigation continues.

---

## 2. Communication Objectives

The crisis communication response is designed to:

1. Establish a single, time-stamped source of truth.
2. Coordinate messages through an approved incident command structure.
3. Distinguish verified facts from assumptions and unknowns.
4. Give each audience relevant information and practical actions.
5. Prevent unauthorized disclosure, speculation, and screenshot sharing.
6. Protect evidence and reduce the risk of further technical harm.
7. Maintain communication through pre-approved out-of-band channels.
8. Connect technical developments to operational and executive decisions.
9. Support legal, regulatory, and contractual assessments.
10. Maintain stakeholder confidence through scheduled, accurate updates.

## 3. Communication Governance

Clear governance is necessary to prevent conflicting statements and unsupported assurances.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Incident Commander | Directs the response and approves operational facts. |
| Communications Lead | Coordinates drafting, approval, distribution, and message consistency. |
| Legal Counsel | Reviews legal, regulatory, contractual, and disclosure wording. |
| Security Lead | Validates technical findings, evidence, and confidence levels. |
| HR Lead | Coordinates workforce communication and acknowledgement tracking. |
| Payroll Lead and Treasury | Confirm payroll dependencies, payment cut-offs, and approved contingency options. |
| Customer or Account Lead | Manages targeted customer communications after impact validation. |
| Relevant Business Owner | Confirms service impact and operational consequences. |

### 3.1 Message Approval Route

1. The responsible technical or business team submits a time-stamped fact.
2. The Communications Lead translates the fact into audience-appropriate language.
3. The Incident Commander validates operational accuracy.
4. Legal Counsel reviews legal, regulatory, and contractual implications.
5. The authorized owner distributes the approved communication.
6. Distribution time, audience, channel, and acknowledgements are recorded.

## 4. Operating Principles

### 4.1 Single Source of Truth

Maintain a time-stamped incident log and an approved situation report. All messages, briefings, and decisions must use this record.

### 4.2 Fact Discipline

Classify information as:

- **Confirmed:** Supported by validated operational or technical evidence.
- **Under investigation:** Plausible but not established.
- **Not yet known:** Information that remains open pending verification.

The organization must not infer the attacker, entry point, root cause, data theft, incident scope, or recovery time.

### 4.3 Need-to-Know Communication

Each audience should receive the information and instructions relevant to its responsibilities. Sensitive technical details should not be shared where disclosure could increase operational, legal, or security risk.

### 4.4 Controlled Disclosure

Employees, contractors, and partners must not publish screenshots, speculate publicly, contact the media, or speak on behalf of the organization unless authorized.

### 4.5 Resilience

If corporate email, file shares, or collaboration platforms are unavailable or suspected to be compromised, communications must move to pre-approved, pre-authenticated out-of-band channels.

### 4.6 Regular Checkpoints

Updates should be issued at stated times, even when there is no material change. Each update should identify verified developments, outstanding unknowns, completed actions, and the next checkpoint.

## 7. Approved Communication Templates

### 7.1 Employee Holding Statement

> At 08:15 today, the organization identified an outage affecting access to shared files used by several teams. Our IT and security teams are investigating the cause and are taking steps to protect systems and preserve evidence. A ransomware incident is one possibility being assessed; it has not yet been confirmed. At this stage, we have not verified the entry point, full scope of affected systems, whether information was accessed or removed, or the expected recovery time.
>
> We are aware that a screenshot is circulating in staff group chats. It is unverified and should not be treated or shared as an official incident update. Please do not forward it, publish incident material, speculate about the cause, or contact customers or the media on the organization's behalf.
>
> Do not attempt to reconnect, restore, or modify affected shared files unless instructed by IT. Continue work using approved unaffected systems and follow your manager's continuity instructions. Report suspicious messages or unusual system behaviour to the IT/Security team using the established fallback contact route.
>
> Payroll is scheduled for Friday. The Payroll and Finance teams are assessing operational dependencies and payment cut-offs; no assurance about processing or timing can be made until that assessment is complete. The next workforce update will be issued at 12:30 through the approved out-of-band channel.

### 7.2 Customer and B2B Partner Update

> We are investigating an operational technology issue identified at 08:15 today that has affected access to some internal shared files. Our IT and security teams are working to establish the cause, determine the scope, and protect the integrity of our systems.
>
> At this time, we have not confirmed whether customer-facing services or customer information are affected. We will contact you directly if our investigation establishes an impact relevant to your organization.
>
> Please continue to use your existing approved service contacts for urgent operational matters. For your protection, do not act on unusual payment, bank-detail, or account-change instructions unless independently verified through your established contact. We will provide the next update by 12:30, or sooner if material verified information requires action.

## 8. Executive Briefing

### 8.1 Confirmed Facts

- At 08:15 on Monday, the critical file share became unavailable.
- Several teams cannot access shared files.
- Payroll processing is scheduled for Friday and may be delayed if access is not restored.
- The security team is preserving evidence and investigating.
- An unverified screenshot is circulating among staff and beyond.

### 8.2 Unknowns Requiring Verification

- Whether ransomware is confirmed.
- The entry point and attacker identity.
- The number and type of affected systems.
- Whether data was accessed, copied, or exposed.
- The availability and integrity of backups.
- The expected recovery time.
- The actual impact on payroll files and processing.
- Which customers, if any, are affected.
- Applicable regulatory and contractual notification duties.

### 8.3 Principal Risks

- Operational disruption and productivity loss.
- Failure to meet payroll or banking cut-offs.
- Loss of evidence or further spread of malicious activity.
- Unsafe employee recovery actions.
- Misinformation and reputational damage.
- Customer concern and potential fraud attempts.
- Legal, regulatory, and contractual exposure.

### 8.4 Decisions Required

Executive leadership should:

1. Approve the incident command structure and authorized spokespersons.
2. Authorize approved out-of-band channels.
3. Confirm business-continuity priorities.
4. Require Payroll and Finance to verify bank cut-offs and contingency options.
5. Direct Legal and Compliance to assess notification obligations.
6. Approve targeted customer communication only after impact validation.

### 8.5 Immediate Decision Triggers

Escalate immediately when a verified event indicates:

- A forecast failure to meet a confirmed payroll or bank cut-off.
- Material unauthorized access or data exfiltration.
- Expansion to additional critical systems.
- Failed containment.
- A time-critical legal, regulatory, or contractual deadline.

### 8.6 Next Executive Update

The next scheduled update is **12:30 on Monday** through the approved out-of-band channel. An earlier briefing is required if a material verified development or decision trigger occurs.

## 9. Channel-Resilience Plan

All alternative channels must come from pre-approved, pre-authenticated continuity arrangements. A new group, telephone number, or contact appearing in the circulating screenshot must not be trusted without independent verification.

| Use | Primary Channel | Fallback Channel | Owner | Receipt and Identity Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incident command | Approved incident bridge independent of affected corporate systems | Pre-arranged telephone conference or direct calls using an offline call tree | Incident Commander | Roll call, role confirmation, known callback number, recorded attendance and time |
| Employees | Approved mass-notification SMS or emergency alert platform | Manager cascade by voice call or physical notice at designated sites | HR Lead | Delivery report, manager roll call, and employee acknowledgement |
| Executives and board | Pre-approved secure emergency messaging or conference bridge | Direct calls to verified executive numbers | Company Secretary or Communications Lead | Decision read-back, recipient confirmation, and decision-log entry |
| Customers and B2B partners | Approved customer-notification channel or verified account contact | Direct call to named contact or verified status page | Customer Lead | Callback to a known number or acknowledgement from the registered contact |
| Bank and payroll partner | Established authenticated portal or verified direct line | Known relationship-manager number from offline records | Payroll Lead or Treasury | Known-number callback, verbal confirmation, and recorded reference and time |
| Legal and regulators | Approved secure legal channel | Verified telephone contact followed by an authorized written notice | Legal Counsel or Compliance Lead | Recipient verification, receipt confirmation, retained transmission record, and approved copy |

### Channel Rule

If a channel's independence or integrity cannot be established, it must be treated as unavailable and replaced with the next approved fallback. Sensitive technical evidence must not be transmitted through ordinary group chats.

## 10. Escalation Threshold and Communication Rationale

A risk-based escalation threshold is appropriate because the incident occurs during a time-critical payroll cycle while the technical facts remain incomplete. The unavailable file share and reports from several teams are confirmed, but ransomware remains a suspected cause rather than an established fact.

The organization must communicate the operational disruption immediately while keeping attribution, incident scope, data exposure, and recovery estimates open until evidence supports a conclusion. Executive escalation should occur when a verified development could change a material business decision, such as a predicted payroll cut-off failure, evidence of data exfiltration, expansion to critical systems, failed containment, or a time-sensitive legal obligation.

The first employee message is issued early because personnel require safe-working instructions and the circulating screenshot creates an immediate misinformation risk. The message acknowledges the screenshot without authenticating it. External customer communication remains limited and should be distributed only after impact and contractual requirements have been checked.

The response intentionally avoids claiming that ransomware is confirmed, naming an attacker or root cause, stating whether data was stolen, quantifying affected systems, promising recovery time, guaranteeing Friday payroll, or making a blanket statement about regulatory notification. Each of those points requires technical, operational, or legal verification.

## 11. Critical Reflection

The most difficult communication dilemma is reducing employee anxiety about Friday payroll without promising an outcome the incident team cannot verify. Employees require timely and clear guidance, especially while an unverified screenshot is spreading and normal file access is disrupted. However, an unsupported assurance that payroll will be processed on time could mislead employees, damage trust, and create legal or operational consequences if a payment cut-off is missed.

The response balances speed and accuracy by issuing an early holding statement that confirms the outage, explains that ransomware is only a possibility under investigation, and gives employees practical actions. It states that Payroll and Finance are assessing dependencies and cut-offs instead of guaranteeing payment. A definite 12:30 update provides a reliable communication checkpoint and reduces the information vacuum in which rumours can spread.

This approach is transparent without being speculative. It communicates what is known, identifies what remains unknown, describes the actions under way, and commits to the next verified update.

## 13. Immediate Verification Actions

### Security and Incident Response

- Validate the outage scope.
- Preserve logs and forensic evidence.
- Assess whether ransomware indicators are present.
- Report findings with time stamps and confidence levels.

### Infrastructure and Backup Team

- Determine backup availability and integrity.
- Avoid actions that could compromise evidence or cause reinfection.
- Identify safe restoration prerequisites.

### Payroll and Finance

- Identify payroll system and file dependencies.
- Confirm exact processing milestones and banking cut-offs.
- Validate approved contingency options.
- Record critical decisions and partner confirmations.

### Legal and Compliance

- Determine applicable jurisdictions and contracts.
- Assess regulatory thresholds and notification deadlines.
- Define evidence-retention and privilege requirements.
- Approve any required notices.

### Communications and Human Resources

- Verify the screenshot's origin only where safe and necessary.
- Publish approved corrections and workforce instructions.
- Maintain a time-stamped distribution and acknowledgement log.
- Monitor stakeholder questions and emerging misinformation.

## 15. Conclusion

The proposed response establishes a disciplined and credible communication structure for a suspected ransomware incident during payroll week. It prioritizes a verified source of truth, audience-specific instructions, resilient communication channels, evidence protection, and decision-led escalation.

Scheduled updates and clearly defined escalation triggers ensure that communication remains active, relevant, and accountable as the technical investigation develops.
