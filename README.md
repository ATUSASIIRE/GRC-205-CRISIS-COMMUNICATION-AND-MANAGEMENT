# GRC205 Crisis Communication and Stakeholder Management

## Ransomware During Payroll Week: Crisis Communication Response Pack

A completed Governance, Risk, and Compliance assignment that presents a structured crisis communication and stakeholder management response for a suspected ransomware incident occurring during a time-critical payroll cycle.

---

## Assignment Overview

This project develops a practical communication response pack for an organization experiencing an outage affecting a critical file share while ransomware remains a suspected, but unconfirmed, cause. The response is designed to support timely communication, protect forensic evidence, limit misinformation, preserve stakeholder confidence, and prevent unsupported claims about payroll, data exposure, incident scope, or recovery time.

The pack applies a fact-based communication model built around three classifications:

- **Confirmed:** Information supported by verified operational or technical evidence.
- **Under investigation:** Possible incident details that have not yet been established.
- **Not yet known:** Information that must remain open until validated by the responsible team.

---

## Scenario

At 08:15 on a Monday, an organization identifies an outage affecting a critical shared-file environment used by several teams. A ransomware incident is under investigation, an unverified screenshot is circulating among employees, and payroll is scheduled for Friday.

The organization must communicate quickly without:

- Confirming ransomware before technical validation.
- Speculating about the attacker, entry point, or root cause.
- Claiming that information was or was not accessed or removed.
- Providing an unsupported recovery estimate.
- Guaranteeing that payroll will be processed on time.
- Making premature regulatory or contractual notification claims.

---

## Objectives

- Establish a single, time-stamped source of truth.
- Coordinate communications through an approved incident command structure.
- Provide audience-specific information and safe-action guidance.
- Control rumours and unauthorized public statements.
- Use resilient, pre-authenticated communication channels.
- Connect technical developments to business decision triggers.
- Maintain regular communication checkpoints, even when there is no material change.
- Preserve evidence and support legal, regulatory, and contractual assessments.

---

## Deliverables

### 1. Incident Communication Plan

Defines the communication purpose, governance model, approval route, operating principles, and the first four-hour response sequence from 08:30 to 12:30.

The sequence covers:

- Incident command alignment.
- Workforce holding communication.
- Executive and critical-partner briefing.
- Customer or B2B client updates.
- A scheduled situation update for relevant stakeholders.

### 2. Stakeholder Map and Management Matrix

Prioritizes stakeholders according to operational dependency, potential harm, and decision authority. For each stakeholder group, the matrix identifies:

- Information requirements.
- Required message or action.
- Communication owner.
- Update cadence.

Stakeholder groups include the direct workforce, executive leadership and board, customers, regulators and legal counsel, banking and payroll partners, and technical incident responders.

### 3. Employee Holding Statement

Provides employees and contractors with an early, controlled update that:

- Confirms the file-share disruption.
- Explains that ransomware is only one possibility under investigation.
- Addresses the unverified screenshot.
- Gives safe-working and incident-reporting instructions.
- Avoids guaranteeing Friday payroll.
- Commits to a defined next update time.

### 4. Customer and B2B Partner Update

Offers a carefully limited external message that explains the operational issue without implying confirmed customer impact or data exposure. It also warns recipients to independently verify unusual payment, bank-detail, or account-change instructions.

### 5. Executive Briefing

Summarizes:

- Confirmed facts.
- Unknowns requiring verification.
- Principal operational, legal, and reputational risks.
- Decisions requiring executive authority.
- Escalation triggers.
- The next scheduled update.

### 6. Channel-Resilience Plan

Defines approved primary and fallback channels for incident command, employees, executives, customers, payroll partners, legal counsel, and regulators.

The plan emphasizes:

- Pre-approved and pre-authenticated channels.
- Known-good callback procedures.
- Identity and receipt verification.
- Offline emergency contact records.
- Avoidance of untrusted group chats.
- Immediate fallback when channel independence or integrity cannot be established.

### 7. Escalation Threshold and Communication Rationale

Uses risk-based escalation to ensure that leadership is alerted when a verified development could change a material business decision.

Key escalation triggers include:

- Forecast failure to meet a confirmed payroll or bank cut-off.
- Evidence of unauthorized access or data exfiltration.
- Expansion to additional critical systems.
- Failed containment.
- A time-critical legal, regulatory, or contractual deadline.

### 8. Critical Reflection

Examines the challenge of reducing employee concern about payroll without providing an assurance that cannot yet be supported. The chosen approach balances speed, accuracy, transparency, and the need for a dependable next communication checkpoint.

### 9. Assumptions, Verification Actions, and Submission Checklist

Separates scenario facts from planning assumptions and assigns immediate verification actions to:

- Security and incident response.
- Infrastructure and backup teams.
- Payroll and Finance.
- Legal and Compliance.
- Communications and Human Resources.

## Communication Governance

The response uses the following accountability structure:

- **Incident Commander:** Approves operational facts and directs the response.
- **Communications Lead:** Coordinates drafting, distribution, and message consistency.
- **Legal Counsel:** Reviews legal, regulatory, and contractual wording.
- **Security Lead:** Validates technical findings and confidence levels.
- **HR Lead:** Coordinates workforce communications and acknowledgements.
- **Payroll Lead and Treasury:** Confirm payroll dependencies, payment cut-offs, and contingency processes.
- **Customer or Account Lead:** Manages tailored client communication after impact validation.

---

## Key Principles Demonstrated

- **Single source of truth:** Decisions and messages rely on an approved incident log and situation report.
- **Accuracy before attribution:** The response distinguishes facts from assumptions and unknowns.
- **Need-to-know communication:** Each audience receives only relevant information and actions.
- **Controlled disclosure:** Only authorized representatives may communicate externally.
- **Evidence protection:** Employees and responders are instructed not to alter affected files or systems.
- **Channel resilience:** Communications continue through verified out-of-band methods when corporate systems are unavailable or untrusted.
- **Decision-led escalation:** Technical findings are connected to operational, financial, legal, and reputational consequences.
- **Scheduled transparency:** Updates are issued at defined checkpoints, including when there is no material change.

---

## Risk Controls

The assignment addresses the following major risks:

- Operational disruption caused by the unavailable file share.
- Failure to meet payroll or banking cut-offs.
- Loss or contamination of forensic evidence.
- Continued spread of malicious activity.
- Employee actions based on rumours or unverified screenshots.
- Customer concern and reputational damage.
- Fraudulent payment or bank-detail change instructions.
- Premature or missed legal, regulatory, and contractual notifications.
- Loss of trust caused by unsupported assurances.
